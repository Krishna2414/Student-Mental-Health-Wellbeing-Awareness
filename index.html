Understanding Student Mental Health

Student life can be rewarding, but it can also bring challenges that affect mental health and wellbeing. Understanding these challenges is an important first step towards looking after yourself and others.

Stress

Deadlines, exams and balancing different responsibilities can sometimes cause stress. Taking breaks and organising tasks can help make responsibilities feel more manageable.

Anxiety

Students may experience worry or nervousness about studies, relationships or their future. Talking to someone you trust can be a useful source of support.

Loneliness

Moving away from home or starting a new course can sometimes feel isolating. Connecting with friends, classmates or student groups can help.

Academic Pressure

Assignments, exams and expectations can create pressure. Creating a realistic study plan and asking for help can make challenges easier to manage.

Simple Ways to Support Your Wellbeing

Small, healthy habits can help support your overall wellbeing. Find approaches that work for you and remember that it is okay to ask for support.

Get Enough Sleep

Try to maintain a regular sleep routine and give yourself time to rest after busy days.

Stay Active

Walking, exercising or taking part in activities you enjoy can be a positive way to look after your physical and mental wellbeing.

Take Regular Breaks

Give yourself short breaks during study sessions. Stepping away from your work can help you return with a clearer mind.

Manage Your Time

Break large tasks into smaller steps and create a realistic schedule for studying, relaxing and spending time with others.

Stay Connected

Spend time with friends, family or other people you trust. Social connections can provide encouragement and a sense of belonging.

Make Time to Relax

Try activities such as reading, listening to music, mindfulness or other hobbies that help you relax.
